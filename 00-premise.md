# Premise  `PROPOSED`

## Title
*The Marriage Startup*

## Logline
A driven, slightly oblivious 28-year-old founder meets a woman who didn't want to
be at the networking event where they met, and over several years discovers that
the hard-won rules of building a company are the same rules for building a
marriage.

## Form
- First-person, past tense, told by the narrator looking back from a few years on
  (lets him be wry about his earlier self and gives the reflections a natural
  home).
- Each chapter = a scene-driven story beat, then a short closing reflection
  (the "Founder's Note", working name) where the narrator names the parallel
  between that stage of the company and that stage of the relationship.
- Romance and startup arcs rise together. When one stalls, the other is
  tested.

## Shape
Roughly 6 parts, one per stage of company life, each with 3-5 chapters
(target ~20-26 chapters). See `outline/stage-map.md`.

## The uncle device
The narrator goes to his retired-founder uncle for advice. He always frames it
as a business question ("How do you handle a co-founder who's pulling away?").
The uncle sees it is really about Maya, lets him keep the pretense, and answers
the question he needs answered. Running gag that deepens into real tenderness.
**The pretense is never spoken aloud.** Both men always know and neither says
it. The payoff comes when Nate decides to propose: Ray asks a few gentle
questions and ends in warm validation, clearly approving and happy for him.

## Ending
The couple marries. In the final scene, Maya tells him she's pregnant. It
lands as the company's next big unknown: a new venture, no playbook, and
everything they learned is about to be tested. Sequel hook.

## Tone
Warm, funny, self-aware, grounded. Silicon Valley satire at the edges but never
a mockery of the characters. Sincere at the center.

## Not this book
- Not a business book with a plot pasted on. The story has to work if you skip
  every reflection.
- Not a story where the relationship is just a metaphor. Maya is a full person
  with her own goals, and sometimes she is right and he is wrong.
