# Style Guide  `PROPOSED`

## Voice
- First person, past tense, retrospective narrator a few years on.
- Dry humor; specific over generic; engineering metaphors used sparingly and
  allowed to fail (Maya can point out when he's doing it).
- Emotion arrives sideways: through objects, actions, what people don't say.

## Reflection sections
- Short: 150-300 words.
- Placed at end of every chapter, set off visually (working label:
  "Founder's Note").
- Must arise from what just happened. No principle without a scene.
- Vary the form: sometimes a list, sometimes a confession, sometimes a
  correction of something he said earlier.
- Avoid clichés ("communication is key"). Find the odd, specific version.

## Dialogue
- Maya: precise, funny, asks the question underneath the question.
- Ray: brief, patient, homespun but not folksy.
- Narrator: over-explains when nervous.

## Locked choices
- Tone: wry and warm.
- Heat: mild / sensual.
- Reflections: set-apart "Founder's Note" at the end of each chapter.
- Companies and programs are fictional; real places are fine.
- Contemporary, lightly dated (few specific tech references).
- Chapters ~3,500-4,500 words; ~24 chapters.

## Guardrails
- Silicon Valley satire should be affectionate.
- Maya must have agency; avoid manic-pixie or long-suffering-wife patterns.
- No infodumps about tech or funding beyond what the scene needs.

## Setup before payoff (DECIDED 2026-09-29)
Any running joke, callback, catchphrase, rule, or piece of shared history **must be
established on the page before a character relies on it.** A reader should never have to
guess what "one" or "the answers" or "a slide" refers to.
- If a line only makes sense with a fact the reader hasn't been given, add the fact first
  or rewrite the line so it explains itself.
- Nate may gloss jargon in a wry parenthetical (e.g., what a "slide" is, what a "term
  sheet" is). That's part of his voice.
- A **deliberate mystery** is fine if the narrator signals that it's one.
- Recurring jokes need a clear first appearance that explains them, then variations.
- Every drafted chapter goes through the **reference-checker** (`.claude/agents/`) before we
  call it approved.
