# Maya Raman: Full Backstory  `PROPOSED`

Age 27 at open. UX researcher at a healthcare nonprofit. Daughter of the family
that runs Raman Family Pharmacy in Fremont.

## The family she came from
- **Suresh Raman (father), ~62.** Pharmacist. Came to the US from Chennai in his
  20s for his pharmacy degree. Worked for a chain for 11 years, hated the
  metrics, saved and opened Raman Family Pharmacy when Maya was 6.
- **Lakshmi Raman (mother), ~59.** Runs the front of the store, the books and
  every customer's business. Knows whose husband is sick and whose son didn't get
  into college. She is the reason people come back.
- **Older brother: Arjun, 31,** an ER resident in Seattle. The "successful one" by
  the family's measure, living far away. The pharmacy pressure lands on Maya by
  default. (Proposed; see questions.)
- Home rule: *family does the work.* Nobody outside the family is trusted with the
  counter, the books, or the future.

## Formative moments
1. **Age 9, doing the books.** Sat at the counter after school doing the receipts
   with Lakshmi. She learned that every column of numbers was a person.
2. **Age 12, the manager.** Her parents once hired a store manager. He skimmed
   from the register for six months and nearly closed the store. Suresh never
   hired again. This is why he refuses to hire help for Maya later: it isn't just
   pride or money, it's a scar.
3. **Age 16, the cough syrup.** A customer, an old man named Mr. Alvarez, couldn't
   read the label. Maya sat with him and rewrote the instructions in large
   type. Suresh let her keep the sheet at the counter, and it became the store's
   handout. She learned that design is care.
4. **Age 19, the choice.** She told her parents she didn't want to be a
   pharmacist. Lakshmi was quiet for two days. Suresh said, "Then do it well,
   whatever it is." They've never fully closed that conversation.
5. **Age 24, Adrian.** Dated a founder for 14 months. He treated her time as
   infinite and her opinions as decoration. He said her job was "cute."
   She left without a fight and told nobody why. She swore off the genre.

## Education and work
- Bachelor's in public health with a design minor at a UC campus.
- Master's in human-centered design (part-time).
- Works at a nonprofit that improves how community clinics talk to patients. She
  interviews patients and staff for a living; she is professionally allergic to
  being managed.

## Her friend Jules
Julia "Jules" Park, her college roommate, now a public defender. Jules gets the
mixer story first, laughs, and says: "You went to a founder mixer on purpose?"
Maya: "My mother made me."

## Psychological profile
- **Core belief:** she's the reliable one and reliable people don't ask for things.
- **Coping:** competence, dry humor, and quietly leaving.
- **Blind spot:** mistakes self-sufficiency for strength. Tests people silently
  and resents them for failing tests they didn't know existed.
- **Tell:** when hurt she gets *polite.*
- **What she's proud of:** she pays attention.
- **What she's afraid of:** needing someone and being met with a solution instead
  of presence, the exact thing Nate does.

## Why she went to the mixer
Lakshmi told her, "You work too much, go out." Then, practical: "Look at these
new technologies. Maybe there's something for the store." Maya, who has spent
years watching software vendors condescend to her parents, went expecting to
hate it. She stood by the exit until Nate asked her, plainly, what her store's
worst problem was, and then listened to the answer.

## Arc beats (backstory tie-ins)
- **Ch 1:** She tests him by lying about the store's worst problem. He asks a
  follow-up anyway. (This is the hook.)
- **Ch 7:** Nate visits the pharmacy; she watches him see the counter as a sales
  channel, then decides he can still learn.
- **Ch 8:** Her deliberate choice to commit, which is harder for her than for him
  because she's chosen before and been disappointed.
- **Ch 15:** Her parents announce the expansion (a second location or an adjacent
  suite) and refuse extra staff. She takes on the double workload without
  complaint, the pattern she always follows.
- **Ch 16:** Nate hands her a "solution." She wanted him to ask, "What do you
  need?"
- **Ch 19:** "You're treating me like a startup." She is not angry, just tired.
- **Ch 24:** The pregnancy: she decides when and how to tell him.
