# The Company

**DECISION (APPROVED): Option A, clinic/pharmacy care-coordination software.**
**Company name (APPROVED): Loopback.health.** Motif: closing the loop, with patients,
with Maya, with himself. Nate says it out loud early; Maya notes he closes every
loop except the ones with people.
Options B and C below are kept for reference only.

Additional consequences of this choice:
- The mixer meet-cute works because Maya's mother sent her to scout technology
  for the family pharmacy.
- Maya knows the customer better than Nate does. Her instincts are product
  feedback he keeps resisting.
- Risk to manage: Nate must not treat her family as a sales lead. That friction
  feeds the "you treat me like a project" conflict (see open question 4).

## Original options (for reference)

## Option A: Clinical scheduling / care-coordination software
- **Pitch:** Software that helps small clinics and pharmacies coordinate patient
  follow-ups.
- **Why it works:** Ties into Maya's world (her family's pharmacy, her work).
  Gives her real expertise and real skepticism. Forces the narrator to learn
  from customers the way he must learn from her.
- **Risk:** Healthcare has heavy regulation. Can be used as texture rather than
  a subject.

## Option B: Developer infrastructure tool
- **Pitch:** A tool that engineers love and executives buy.
- **Why it works:** Classic Valley story; easy to depict; lots of comedy.
- **Risk:** Abstract and disconnected from Maya's world. Fewer places for the
  product to comment on the relationship.

## Option C: A consumer app about... communication
- **Pitch:** Something to help people stay connected.
- **Why it works:** Direct irony (a founder who builds communication tools and
  can't communicate).
- **Risk:** Too on-the-nose unless played with a light touch.

**Recommendation:** Option A. But this is your call.

## Company beats (product-agnostic)
1. **Idea/inception:** A spark from a customer's actual pain.
2. **Founding team:** Narrator + co-founder.
3. **Prototype:** Ugly, works for one user.
4. **Early users:** Small wins, first real feedback.
5. **First paying customers.**
6. **Seed round:** Pitching, term sheets, dilution, obligations.
7. **Team build:** Hiring, culture, first departure.
8. **Growth:** Metrics rise, intimacy falls.
9. **Crisis:** A major customer leaves, a bad hire, or a co-founder conflict.
   The company nearly dies.
10. **Recovery/pivot.**
11. **Series A / stability.**
12. **Next horizon:** New product, new risks (the sequel hook).
