## 1. "A loop with no escalation, and the stakes never bite"

**Problem.** Nate applies a system to a person, someone names it, he's ashamed, he says the plain thing, Maya answers "Okay." The chapter-shaped mistake is predictable. Conflicts resolve inside a chapter. The business side has no friction.

**Actions**
1. **One system-mistake per act (S–M each).** Keep Ch 2 (spreadsheet), Ch 5 (funnel), Ch 7 (leads) as Act 1. Act 2: keep Ch 13's reminder and fold the staffing sheet and lease row into one short beat. Act 3: compress Ch 17's Master Plan to a paragraph and reframe it (see section 4). Cut Ch 20's calendar-invite scene to a callback inside Ch 21. Keep Ch 21's "I could set a reminder" as a one-line gag, since it now follows the foot-down.
2. **Give the Ch 18 incident a cost (M).** Candidates, pick two: (a) Carla must sign formal notices to the 31 patients and takes a hit with her VP; (b) Meridian invokes the Section 9 review early and puts Loopback on a 30-day probation that shapes the wedding-day go-live; (c) the pharmacist Gerald is disciplined, and Esperanza quietly takes the weight; (d) Nate must personally call the "DV" patient's current number holder and apologize. Replace "it was luck" with a cost that stays on the page.
3. **A real cash squeeze (S).** Ch 11–12: put a number on runway before Margo ("eleven weeks"), so Sable's globe has real pull. Show Nate nearly say yes.
4. **Test the forty-minute rule at cost (M, Ch 12).** Sable demands San Francisco three days a week. Maya says "do what's right for the company." He nearly takes it. That shows the cost of both Nate's yes and her silence, and it sets up Ch 23's "say it at forty minutes, not forty-one."
5. **Optional bigger swing (L, Decision D3-A).** Priya actually leaves after Ch 14 for ~10 days. One night alert goes unanswered with a real consequence (Dr. Okafor's patient, not Meridian's). Ch 15's forty-five-minute sit then pays a real debt.

**Leave alone.** The clock night (Ch 10) and the DV/Carla telling-within-the-hour (Ch 18). They work because they have teeth.

**Test.** By Ch 14 a reader should think the company or the marriage could actually break, and one loss should still be on the board at Ch 20.

**Effort.** M–L. **Risk.** Changes in Ch 18 ripple into Ch 19–23, and I'd rerun the checker on 18–23.

## 3. "Nate: likable but passive and over-explained; tics everywhere"

**Actions**
1. **Agency audit (S).** List what Nate *does* with consequences per chapter. Fix flat spots with a decision he makes: Ch 13 (he walks out on the spreadsheet, rather than being "taught"), Ch 15 (he asks Priya something that costs him), Ch 20/21 (he makes the Saturday-line change himself rather than being handed it).
2. **Reduce "the test question" (M).** Every wise woman asks him a test and he passes by shutting up. Keep it in Ch 5 and 15; elsewhere let him answer clumsily or push back once (Ch 9 and 23 now have this).
3. **Remove self-grading and foreshadow crutches (S per chapter).** "The hardest thing I did that year," "Remember that, later," "I'll come back to it," "the best work of my career." Search regexes; keep ≤ 5 in the whole book.
4. **Tic budgets (M overall).** Current estimate → target: "I'd like to say / the record / I want it noted" ~75 → ≤ 8; "the way you'd / like a man who…" ~100 → ≤ 20; "Hm" 100+ → ≤ 25 (Ray and Lakshmi only); "a thing" 89 → ≤ 30; "very" 63+ → ≤ 25; flat-hand gestures ~50 → ≤ 12; "quarter turn" 12 → ≤ 4; coat mentions 72 → ~35 (keep the coat as motif at key beats); stock images ("put the smile away like a receipt," "adding a number to a column," "plain gray type of a thing built in a hurry") → one use each.
5. **Process.** I'll script the counts per chapter, then edit one chapter at a time with a before/after table, and rerun the counts at the end. Ch 1 is approved, so its changes come with a diff for your sign-off.

**Test.** Count budgets met; a cold reader can't predict a character's next line from the previous page.

**Effort.** L (it touches all 24 chapters, but mostly sentence-level).
